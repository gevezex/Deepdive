Why we sleep less than our grandparents did

Door Maria Lindqvist · 14 minuten lezen

Every few years a headline announces that we are in the grip of a sleep epidemic. The story is familiar: screens, stress and long commuting hours have eaten away at the night, and we are paying for it with our health.

Yet the best long-term data tell a quieter story. Average sleep duration has barely moved since 1960, and in some countries it has even risen slightly. What changed is not how long we sleep, but when.

Artificial light pushed sleep later, not shorter, and the alarm clock kept the morning where it was. The result feels like less sleep, because it is less sleep on the nights that matter most to us.
